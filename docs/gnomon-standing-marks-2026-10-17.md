# Gnomon: standing dates in both hemispheres

The captured Sydney analemmatic drawing called its northern standing mark
Summer and its southern mark Winter. Those names described Northern Hemisphere
seasons: following local summer put the rod 64.7997 mm from the correct point
on the 90 mm layout. The new drawing names June and December instead. Its
geometry and existing portable design files retain their values.

The setup card also keeps an analemmatic rod vertical, with a plumb-line check,
while polar dials retain their polar-style directions. The north-finding note
now qualifies Polaris as a Northern Hemisphere reference. Instructions refer
to marked dates rather than implying that every calendar day is labelled.

[Native reproduction and construction guide](https://github.com/antonsoo/gnomon/tree/main/studies/seasonal-marks)
retain the old/new SVGs, a portable Sydney file, independent ideal Sun-vector
checks (eight wrong standing directions become zero across the 16 cases), and
complete design comparisons. These are controlled geometry checks, not a new
ephemeris benchmark or measurements from a built sundial.

The archived build passed lint, type checking, all 304 unit checks and 68 actual
Chromium/Firefox browser workflows. Its 33 built files and canonical root URL
match the public designer. Four public desktop/mobile contexts open the saved
setup after disconnecting, download/reopen its SVG, and save identical inputs.
They then switch to a horizontal dial and download their first A4 and Letter
kits offline; independent PDF inspection verifies eight files, 16 pages and
16 physical 100 mm calibration bars.
